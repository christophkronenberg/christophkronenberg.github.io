I teach economics as a tool for answering policy questions. Students should leave my courses able to reason about a tax, a regulation or a social insurance rule with economic theory, and then confront that reasoning with data. I teach theory and evidence together and treat causal inference as a skill every student can learn, whatever their mathematical background. In lectures I use peer instruction: students answer a conceptual question, discuss it in pairs and answer again, which shows me misconceptions while there is still time to address them. Students get low-stakes feedback on problem sets before any graded exam. Many students at the University of Duisburg-Essen are the first in their family to attend university, so I draw on examples from their own region and lives, and I show the researchers behind the papers we discuss, because role models shape who chooses to study economics. I teach in German at bachelor's level and mostly in English at master's level.
