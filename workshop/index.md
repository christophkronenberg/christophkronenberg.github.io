I founded the Economics of Mental Health Workshop in Essen in 2018 and have organized it every year since. It brings together economists and researchers from neighboring fields who study mental health.

## Who should submit

We strongly encourage junior researchers to submit, including doctoral students. We also invite submissions from scholars outside economics who work on the economics of mental health, for example in psychology, public health or health services research. Empirical work is especially welcome; theoretical work is welcome too. The workshop is not the right venue for cost-effectiveness analyses, pure costing analyses or qualitative work.

## Format

The workshop follows the study-group format used for over 50 years by the [UK Health Economists' Study Group](https://hesg.org.uk/about/), for about 40 years by the Nordic Health Economists' Study Group, and more recently by the Lowlands Health Economists' Study Group and other venues.

- Discussants, not authors, present and discuss each paper. The discussant decides whether presentation and discussion run sequentially or together.
- All participants are expected to have at least skimmed all papers.
- Only the version circulated to all participants is discussed; discussants and authors do not exchange updated drafts.
- Authors then have a few minutes to correct anything the discussant unintentionally misrepresented.
- The audience follows with critical and constructive feedback.
- The session chair enforces time limits, keeps the discussion going (prepared questions help), and keeps questions constructive and on topic.

Keynotes are presentations followed by Q&A and do not follow this format.
